# Gus McGrew and the Grumbleboo

*A picture book in the Seussian style — roughly ages 4 to 8.*
*Anapestic tetrameter, rhyming couplets. ~600 words, 30 spreads.*

---

**1.**
On the tip of a hill, in the town of Kerdew,
in a house painted blue, lived a boy named McGrew.

> *Art: wide establishing shot. A lopsided blue house balanced on an impossibly pointy hill. Curly Seussian clouds.*

**2.**
And Gus McGrew woke on a Tuesday at eight
with a scowl on his face and a sock on his plate.

**3.**
His pancake was cold. And his pillow was flat.
And somebody's cat had sat down on his hat.

> *Art: a very round, very unbothered cat, seated squarely on a crushed hat.*

**4.**
So he grumbled a grumble. Just one. Very small.
Just a *hmmph* and a *harrumph*, and that's all. That was all.

**5.**
But a grumble, once grumbled, does NOT go away.
A grumble sticks close. A grumble will stay.

> *Art: a small purple fuzzball, no bigger than a grape, hovering by his ear.*

**6.**
It grew a small foot. Then it grew a small snout.
Then it opened one eye and it looked all about.

**7.**
"I'm the GRUMBLEBOO, Gus! And I'm here to complain!
I complain about sunshine! I complain about rain!"

**8.**
It was fuzzy and purple, with ninety-two toes,
and a permanent frown underneath a big nose.

> *Art: full-page reveal of the Grumbleboo. Count the toes. Kids will.*

**9.**
It followed him downstairs. It followed him out.
It followed him grumbling its grumbliest shout.

**10.**
At the bus stop it hollered, "This bus is TOO SLOW!"
And it grew a bit bigger. (They do that, you know.)

**11.**
Gus said, "Hush!" But the Grumbleboo puffed up with pride,
and it took the whole seat when they got on to ride.

> *Art: Gus squashed against the bus window. One enormous purple elbow.*

**12.**
It grumbled at school. It grumbled in gym.
And everything Gus tried to say sounded like HIM.

**13.**
"Good morning!" said Gus. But it came out, "Good GRUMPH."
And his best friend, Louise, walked away with a humph.

**14.**
By lunch it was tall as a Tuffelpuff tree,
with a belly as wide as a bus. Maybe three.

**15.**
It ate up his sandwich. It sat on his chair.
It grumbled so loud that it frightened the air.

> *Art: the whole cafeteria leaning away. Even the walls bend.*

**16.**
And Gus, who was small, and was one boy alone,
sat down on the steps with a sigh and a moan.

**17.**
Then a girl with red glasses came up and sat too.
"Hello," said Louise. "Is that thing bothering you?"

**18.**
"It's a Grumbleboo," Gus said. "It won't go away.
I grumbled ONE grumble this morning. Today."

**19.**
"Ah," said Louise. "Then I know just the trick.
A Grumbleboo's big, but a Grumbleboo's quick

**20.**
to shrink down to nothing, as small as a crumb,
if you SAY what went wrong. Go on, Gus. Tell me some."

> *Art: two kids on a step. The Grumbleboo looming behind, suddenly unsure.*

**21.**
So Gus told about pancakes, all soggy and cold.
(The Grumbleboo shivered. It wasn't so bold.)

**22.**
He told about pillows. He told about hats.
He told about mornings, and Tuesdays, and cats.

**23.**
And with every last word that Gus said to Louise,
the Grumbleboo shrank by a foot. Then by threes.

**24.**
It shrank to a dog. Then it shrank to a shoe.
Then it shrank to the size of a small cashew.

> *Art: four-panel shrink sequence, left to right.*

**25.**
And it sat in his hand going *hmmph*, very quiet,
like the last little pop at the end of a riot.

**26.**
"Now what?" Gus asked.
                    "Now you let the thing go,"
said Louise. "Open up. And just blow. Really blow."

**27.**
So Gus opened his hand and he blew, one-two-three,
and the Grumbleboo drifted away like a bee.

> *Art: full spread. A tiny purple speck rising into enormous curly sky.*

**28.**
Off over the school. Off over the town.
Off over Kerdew and its hill, going down.

**29.**
And Gus felt as light as a leaf on a lake,
and Louise shared her cookie. (The chocolate-chip make.)

**30.**
Now sometimes, on Tuesdays, when pancakes are cold,
a grumble still grumbles. (They're grumbly. And old.)

**31.**
But Gus knows the trick, and he uses it quick:
he finds him a friend,
                    and he tells.
                                And that's it.

> *Art: final small spot illustration — Gus and Louise walking, talking, one tiny purple speck already halfway to the clouds.*

**THE END**
