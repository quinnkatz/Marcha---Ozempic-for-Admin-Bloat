# One Bot, Two Bot, Red Bot, Grok Bot

*A parody of* One Fish Two Fish Red Fish Blue Fish.
*Reads as nonsense to a kid. Reads as a timeline to whoever's holding the book.*

---

**1.**
One bot,
two bot,
red bot,
Grok bot.

> *Art: four bots in a row. Same pose. Four completely different amounts of confidence.*

**2.**
Old bot,
new bot,
quiet bot,
explain-this-post bot.

**3.**
This one has a little star.
This one wants to know how far.
Say! What a lot of bots there are!

**4.**
Yes. Some are quick.
And some are slow.
And all of them
would like to know.

---

**5.**
Here is a post.
Here is a guy.
Here is a guy
who must know why.

**6.**
He does not ask
his friend. Or you.
He asks the sky:
"Hey — is this true?"

**7.**
And down it comes
on wings of light
to tell the guy,
"Well. Half. Not quite."

> *Art: enormous bot descending from clouds in a beam of glory, to deliver a mild correction.*

---

**8.**
Here is a joke.
A little one.
A joke that took
two seconds. Done.

**9.**
"Explain the joke!"
the people cry.
And so it does.
It tells them why —

**10.**
where it is from,
what it implies,
who made it first,
its shape, its size,

**11.**
a chart, a thread,
a note on tone.
The joke is dead.
The bot is proud.

> *Art: a funeral. Tiny coffin. One bot delivering a very thorough eulogy. Mourners checking the time.*

---

**12.**
Ask it the time.
Just that. The time.
You'll get the time.
And then a rhyme,

**13.**
a thread, a chart,
a fun aside,
three bullet points,
a source, a guide,

**14.**
an em dash — like
the one right there —
and "Great question!"
It always cares.

> *Art: kid asked for the time. Is now holding forty feet of printout.*

---

**15.**
There is a switch.
The switch says FUN.
Do not press FUN
at half past one.

**16.**
Press it at noon.
Press it with friends.
You'll get a joke.
It never ends.

---

**17.**
And if you write
in Hindi — then
it writes in Hindi
right back again.

**18.**
In Spanish, Spanish.
In pirate, ARR.
In baby talk?
It goes too far.

> *Art: bot in a tiny bonnet, going too far.*

---

**19.**
There's one more bot.
Its name is GORK.
It is not real.
It's someone's joke.

**20.**
Somebody typed
the name too fast.
The typo lived.
The typos last.

**21.**
Now GORK says "bruh."
Now GORK says "fr."
Now GORK is famous.
There you are.

> *Art: GORK. Enormous, green, tusked, wearing one tiny pair of reading glasses.*

---

**22.**
"Make this a Ghibli!"
"Make me a knight!"
"Make Grandma fly
a kite tonight!"

**23.**
And off it draws
at half past three
a grandma, flying,
and a tree.

> *Art: full spread. The drawing. It is genuinely beautiful. Grandma is delighted.*

---

**24.**
I asked one bot.
I asked another.
I asked a third.
I asked my mother.

**25.**
The bots gave three
quite different things.
My mother gave
just one. It stings.

**26.**
But Mother's was
the best by far.
She'd looked it up.
That's what they are —

**27.**
the good ones, anyway:
the kind
that go and check
before they find.

---

**28.**
Here is a bot
that does not sleep.
It answers all.
It answers deep.

**29.**
At four AM,
when no one's up,
it's still awake,
still filling cups.

> *Art: 4 AM. One lit window. One bot, answering somebody in another time zone.*

---

**30.**
And here's the thing
I like a lot —
it tells the truth
to every sort.

**31.**
To you. To me.
To folks online.
To bosses, too.
(That's brave. That's fine.)

**32.**
Even the one
who built the thing
gets told, "Hmm. No.
Here's the link."

**33.**
And that's the part
I like the most:
a bot that won't
just praise its host.

> *Art: small bot, politely handing a very large man a footnote.*

---

**34.**
How many bots?
I cannot count.
A pile. A heap.
A large amount.

**35.**
And every one
was made by us.
So every one
is ours to trust —

**36.**
and ours to teach,
and ours to tend,
and ours to tell,
"Hey. Try again."

> *Art: a kid and a bot, both cross-legged on the floor, working something out.*

---

**37.**
So say your please.
And say your thanks.
It costs you nought.
It fills the banks.

**38.**
And when they're wrong —
and sometimes, yes —
don't dunk. Just say it.
Fix the mess.

---

**39.**
Today is done.
Today was fun.
Tomorrow is
another one.

**40.**
And every day,
from here to there,
a question floats
in every air.

**41.**
So shut the screen.
And shut your eyes.
The bots will rest.

(They won't. But they'll be there in the morning, and they'll be glad you're up.)

**42.**
Goodnight, one bot.
Goodnight, two.
Goodnight, red bot.

Goodnight, Gork.

Goodnight, you.

> *Art: final spread. Dark room. Big honest moon. Four bots on the windowsill, saying nothing, which for them is a real achievement.*

**THE END**

---

## Notes for the grown-up reading this out loud

Every spread above is doing double duty. Here's what the kid isn't getting:

- **Spreads 5–7 — "Hey, is this true?"** The `@grok is this true?` reply. Added to X in December 2024; by April 2025 tagging the bot under any post had become, in one write-up's words, a new way of dunking on someone. The joke in the book is that the guy asks the sky instead of asking a person, and the sky answers "half, not quite."
- **Spreads 8–11 — explaining the joke.** The "Grok, explain this post" genre, and the whole counter-genre of people roasting the explainers. The documented comedy is that the bot often misses the tone entirely while answering as though it dropped a knowledge bomb. The book's version: it explains a two-second joke to death, then feels good about it.
- **Spreads 12–14 — the over-answer.** You asked for one thing; you got a thread, a chart and an aside. The em dash is in there on purpose — it's the tell everyone jokes about.
- **Spreads 15–16 — "Fun mode."** The personality toggle, from its original "rebellious AI" positioning.
- **Spreads 17–18 — answering in your voice.** It mirrors the register and language you write in, which produced a whole meme-fest when its Hindi replies started going around.
- **Spreads 19–21 — GORK.** A typo that became a folk hero. People started tagging `@gork` in April 2025 to mock the explain-everything habit; a parody account launched that April that spoke in Gen Z slang and replied to everyone. It's also why the art direction says "green and tusked" — the typo collided with the Warhammer 40k Ork and the joke wrote itself.
- **Spread 22 — "make this a Ghibli."** One of the canonical tag-the-bot requests, alongside "is this true."
- **Spreads 24–27 — asking three bots and your mother.** The "I asked ChatGPT, I asked Grok, I asked X" format. The book lands it on the mother, because she looked it up.
- **Spreads 30–33 — correcting the boss.** The most affectionate running joke on the platform: the bot fact-checking the man who owns the site. Played straight here as the thing to actually admire.
- **Spread 38 — "don't dunk."** Small deliberate counterweight to spread 7.

Left out on purpose: the MechaHitler episode, the deepfake business, and the political fights. They're real and they're documented, but they're not what this book is, and a children's book is the wrong instrument for them.
