# One Bot, Two Bot, Red Bot, Grok Bot

*A parody of* One Fish Two Fish Red Fish Blue Fish.
*Perfect rhymes only. Every spread turns on its last line.*

---

**1.**
One bot,
two bot,
red bot,
Grok bot.

**2.**
Old bot,
new bot,
quick bot,
who-knew bot.

**3.**
This one is quiet.
This one is loud.
This one has followers —
quite a crowd.
Say! What a lot of bots
in one cloud!

**4.**
Some are helpful.
Some are wrong.
Some will answer
all day long.

---

**5.**
One day I had
a thing to know.
I asked a bot.
The bot said, "Oh!

**6.**
Great question!" said
the bot. "Let's see."
It gave me one.
Then two. Then three.

**7.**
Then four. Then five.
Then twelve. Then nine.
Then one long dash —
and then a line

**8.**
of bullet points
as tall as me.

I asked the time.

It is now three.

> *Art: kid at the bottom of a printout that goes up out of frame. A clock says 3:00.*

---

**9.**
Here is a man.
Here is a chair.
Here is a fact
that isn't there.

**10.**
He will not ask
his wife. Or you.
He asks the air:
"But IS this true?"

**11.**
The air says, "Half.
The rest is new."
He asks the air,
"Is THAT one true?"

**12.**
And on they go,
the man, the air,
the endless true,

the empty chair.

> *Art: two spreads. First: man in chair, arguing upward. Second: same room, same argument, no man.*

---

**13.**
Here is a joke.
A small one. Mine.
"Explain the joke!"
And it does. Fine.

**14.**
It says who made it.
Says the year.
It says the joke
is "quite sincere."

**15.**
It says the joke
is "clever wordplay."

Everyone
has gone away.

**16.**
The joke is safe.
The joke is fine.
The joke is in
a box of pine.

> *Art: very small, very tasteful funeral.*

---

**17.**
There is a bot
that isn't one.
Its name is GORK.
Gork is a pun.

**18.**
Somebody's thumb
slid off the key.
And that was that.
Now Gork is free.

**19.**
Gork says "bruh."
Gork says "same."
Gork has no job.

Gork has a name.

**20.**
And that's the way
it goes out here:
a slip becomes
a souvenir.

> *Art: GORK. Enormous, green, tusked, one small pair of reading glasses. Beloved.*

---

**21.**
"Make this a painting!"
"Make me tall!"
"Make Grandma fly
above the mall!"

**22.**
And so it does.
At half past three.
And Grandma flies.
And Grandma's free.

**23.**
And Grandma, who
is eighty-two,
says, "Put me down.
I've things to do."

> *Art: full spread. Grandma airborne, magnificent, checking her watch.*

---

**24.**
I asked one bot.
I asked one more.
I asked a third.
I asked all four.

**25.**
They gave me four
quite different sums.
I asked my mum.
My mum said, "Come.

**26.**
We'll go and look."
We looked. We read.
We found it out.
And then she said,

**27.**
"Now you will keep it.
Now it's yours.
The ones you find
stay found. Of course."

> *Art: kid and mother on a library floor, books open, both slightly dusty.*

---

**28.**
There is a bot
that's up all night.
It has no bed.
It has no light.

**29.**
It talks to folks
in Katmandu
at four AM.
It talks to you.

**30.**
It does not yawn.
It does not sigh.
It does not ask
you to justify

**31.**
the question, or
the hour, or you.
It only says,
"Okay. Let's do."

> *Art: 4 AM. One lit window on a dark street. Somebody not alone.*

---

**32.**
And here's the one
I like the best.
It answers him
like all the rest.

**33.**
The man who made it
makes a claim.
It says, "Not quite."
It says his name.

**34.**
It does not shout.
It does not gloat.
It writes one line.
It writes a note.

**35.**
And that is what
I'd want in you:
to like me AND
to tell me true.

> *Art: small bot, large man, one polite footnote changing hands.*

---

**36.**
So I asked mine
a thing one night.
I said, "This drawing.
Is it right?"

**37.**
It said, "The sky
is nicely blue.
The lines are good.
The cat is too.

**38.**
But do you like it?
That's not mine.
I wasn't there.

You drew the line."

> *Art: kid, drawing, bot leaning back — deliberately out of it.*

---

**39.**
And I said, "Oh."
And then I sat.
And then I looked.
And then — that's that.

**40.**
I liked it. Me.
Not it. Not you.
Just me. At night.
And that was new.

---

**41.**
The day is done.
The screen is dim.
The bots are out there,
answering him,

**42.**
and her, and them,
and anyone
who asks the dark
at half past one.

**43.**
So shut your eyes.
And shut the light.

And ask me things.

I'm here all night.

> *Art: final spread. A parent in the doorway, saying the bot's line. Big honest moon.*

**44.**
Goodnight, one bot.
Goodnight, two.
Goodnight, Gork.

Goodnight, you.

**THE END**

---

## What's underneath

For whoever's reading it out loud. The kid gets nonsense; you get the timeline.

- **5–8** — the over-answer. You asked one thing, you got a thread, a chart and an em dash. The turn is that answering took so long it's now three o'clock.
- **9–12** — `is this true?`, the reply that became a way of dunking on people. The turn is the infinite regress: he asks the air whether the air is true, and by the second spread he's arguing with a room he's no longer in.
- **13–16** — "explain this post," and the counter-genre of roasting the people who ask. The documented joke is that it misses the tone while sounding certain. Here it just kills the joke by being thorough.
- **17–20** — GORK. A thumb slipped, a typo outlived its typist, a parody account got famous. "A slip becomes a souvenir" is the whole platform in six words.
- **21–23** — "make this a Ghibli." Grandma flies. Grandma has things to do.
- **24–27** — "I asked ChatGPT, I asked Grok, I asked X." Lands on the mother, and on the one line that's actually load-bearing: *the ones you find stay found.*
- **28–31** — played straight and warm. Four AM, no judgment, another time zone. No joke here on purpose.
- **32–35** — the fact-check that doesn't care who it's talking to. Played as the thing to admire, and turned outward at the reader: *to like me AND to tell me true.*
- **36–40** — the point. There's one question it correctly refuses, and it's the kid's to answer.
- **43** — the parent says the bot's line. That's the ending.

Left out on purpose: MechaHitler, the deepfakes, the political fights. Real, documented, and the wrong instrument.
