Medspas · Dental · Outpatient practices

If you see patients, every tool has to sign on the dotted line.

Patient information is protected health information, or PHI. That covers a name next to a treatment, a before-and-after photo, an allergy note, and an appointment for a specific service. Under HIPAA, any outside company that stores or handles PHI for your practice is a business associate. That includes your booking software, your phone and texting system, an AI note-taker, and consultants like us. A business associate has to sign a Business Associate Agreement (BAA) before it touches that information.

A BAA is a contract that says what the vendor may and may not do with your patients’ information, how it must protect that information, and what it must tell you if something goes wrong. If a tool that holds your patients’ information won’t sign one, we don’t recommend it for clinical use.

How we handle patient information.

  • We sign a BAA with you before we see any report that contains patient information.

  • We usually don’t need names at all. Counts and totals, such as missed calls, no-shows, and unfilled cancellations, show us where the money is going. We ask for de-identified or summary reports first and only ask for more if we genuinely need it.

  • Patient information never goes into consumer AI. That means no regular ChatGPT, no free note-takers, and no personal accounts. If we use AI to analyze a report, the report is de-identified first or the tool is covered by a BAA.

  • We only recommend clinical tools that will sign a BAA, and we tell you which plan you need. If the plan you’re on doesn’t include one, your written Marcha plan will say so.

  • A clinician signs off on every AI-drafted note. AI note-takers can draft a note, but a licensed clinician reviews and signs it. We never recommend tools that diagnose.

Not sure HIPAA applies to you?

Whether your practice is technically a HIPAA “covered entity” depends partly on how you bill. A practice that sends insurance claims or other standard transactions electronically is covered. Most dental offices and insurance-billing practices fall into that group. A cash-only medspa may not be. Florida law still requires licensed practitioners to keep patient records confidential, though, and Florida’s data breach law counts medical information as protected personal information. Our approach doesn’t change either way: we handle patient information as if HIPAA applies. If you’re not a covered entity, we’ll still put the same protections in a written confidentiality agreement.

Studios · Salons · Every business we work with

Your clients still care where their information goes.

Your booking system knows more about your clients than you might think. It holds their names, phone numbers, birthdays, and cards on file, and it often holds more personal details too: an injury noted on a Pilates intake form, a pregnancy, or an allergy flagged before a lash or gel appointment. Your clients shared that with you, not with a software company.

Clients don’t read vendor terms of service. If a tool you chose mishandles their information, they won’t blame the vendor. They’ll blame the studio they trusted. Your members, guests, and regulars come back because they feel looked after, and that includes their data.

HIPAA probably doesn’t apply to your studio or salon, but other rules still do. The Federal Trade Commission expects businesses to be honest about how they handle people’s information. Florida law requires businesses to take reasonable steps to protect personal information and to notify people after certain data breaches. You don’t need to become an expert in any of this. You just need tools you can trust with your clients’ information, and that’s what we check.

What that looks like for you.

  • Before we recommend any tool, we check its data terms. We look at whether it trains AI on your client data, where that data is stored, who at the company can see it, and whether you can export it and delete it if you leave.

  • We favor switching on features you already pay for. Your booking platform already holds your client data under an agreement you’ve signed. Turning on a feature inside it usually means your data goes to fewer new places.

  • If a fix includes automatic texts, we set them up the right way. Reminders go to clients who gave you their number for appointments, marketing messages go only to clients who opted in, and anyone can reply STOP.

  • We’ll tell you plainly when a tool isn’t worth the risk, even if it’s popular.

What we promise, in plain words.

These are the practices we follow with every client, clinical or not.

  1. 01

    We ask for the least information we need.

    Reports with totals come first. Names come only if they’re truly necessary.

  2. 02

    We sign before we look.

    Clinical clients get a BAA. Everyone else gets a confidentiality agreement. Both are signed before we open a single report.

  3. 03

    Your information stays in accounts built for it.

    Files live in our business Google Workspace account under Google’s BAA, are shared only with the named people who need them, and never go through personal accounts or consumer AI tools.

  4. 04

    Access is limited and temporary.

    If we need to see inside your software, we ask for our own login at the lowest access level that works, never a shared password. At the end of the engagement, you remove our access and we confirm it’s gone.

  5. 05

    We delete what we no longer need.

    Within 30 days of delivering your plan, we delete or return the reports you shared, unless you ask us in writing to keep them longer.

  6. 06

    We never sell your data, never use it to train AI, and never contact your clients.

  7. 07

    If something goes wrong, you’ll hear it from us quickly.

    If we learn that information you shared with us may have been exposed, we’ll tell you promptly and in writing so you can meet your own obligations.

Our call notes.

Our 45-minute listening call uses AI note-taking only if everyone on the call agrees. Florida requires everyone’s consent before a conversation is recorded, and we’d ask for it anyway. We’ll ask before the call and again when it starts. If you’d rather not, just say so. We’ll take notes by hand and nothing about the assessment changes. We use the notes only to write your plan. Recordings are deleted within 7 days, once the notes are written. Please don’t share names on the call. We don’t need them.

Our checklist, before anything reaches your plan.

  • Will they sign a BAA, and on which plan? (clinical clients)
  • Do they train AI on your data? Does that include “de-identified” data, and can you turn it off?
  • Where is your data stored, and who at the vendor can see it?
  • Can you control what each staff member sees, and check who looked at what?
  • Is your data encrypted, and does the tool support two-step login?
  • Which other companies touch your data? That includes outside AI models, and whether they’re covered too.
  • What happens to recordings and transcripts, and for how long?
  • Can you export everything and delete it if you leave?
  • For texting tools: are consent and opt-outs handled automatically?
  • Will they tell you before they change how they use your data?

The answers go into your written plan, so you can see why we chose each tool.

FAQ

Before we look.

These are the questions we hear before we look at a single report.

Can I paste health details into ChatGPT?

No software meets HIPAA on its own. What matters is whether the company signs a BAA and how you use the tool. OpenAI doesn’t offer a BAA for free ChatGPT, Plus, Pro, or ChatGPT Business. It offers one for sales-managed ChatGPT Enterprise and Edu accounts, ChatGPT for Healthcare, and its API. Claude works the same way: HIPAA-ready Enterprise plans and the API are covered, and the Free, Pro, Max, and Team plans aren’t. So please don’t paste health details about the people you care for into the regular ChatGPT or Claude apps. If AI would genuinely help your practice, we’ll point you to a covered option or to a feature inside software you already use.

My studio isn’t medical. Why does this matter?

Because your clients still trust you with personal information, including their phone numbers, cards on file, and sometimes injuries or health notes from an intake form. If a tool mishandles it, your clients will see it as your studio’s mistake. Florida law also expects businesses to protect personal information and to notify people after certain breaches. We check each tool’s data terms so you don’t have to read the fine print yourself.

Will you see my client list?

Usually not. To find where money leaks, we mostly need totals, such as missed calls, no-shows, empty slots, and clients who haven’t been back. If a named list would help, for example to size a win-back campaign, we’ll explain why and ask first. Clinical clients sign a BAA before we see anything with names.

What happens to our call notes?

We only use AI note-taking if everyone on the call agrees. Otherwise we take notes by hand. The notes are used to write your plan and nothing else. Recordings are deleted within 7 days, and written notes are deleted within 30 days of delivering your plan unless you ask us to keep them.

Do we need to sign a BAA with you?

If you’re a medspa, dental office, or practice and we’ll see any report containing health information tied to a person, yes, and we’ll send it to you before the work starts. If we only look at de-identified totals, a BAA may not be required, but we’re glad to sign one anyway.

Is there an official HIPAA certification?

There’s no official HIPAA certification. The U.S. Department of Health and Human Services doesn’t recognize private certifications, so be careful with anyone who claims one. What we can offer is a signed BAA, clear written practices, and honest answers about every tool we recommend.

Is this legal advice?

No. We help you choose and set up tools carefully, and we’ll flag questions that belong with your attorney.
