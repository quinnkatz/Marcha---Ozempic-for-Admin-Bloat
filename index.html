The problem

You know exactly what's wrong with your week

The inquiry that came in at 9pm and sat until morning, by which point she booked down the street. The consult who said "let me think about it" five weeks ago and never heard from anyone again — a treatment that quietly became nothing. The Friday no-show you can't resell at four hours' notice. The same five questions, answered by hand, forever.

None of that is a mystery to you. What you don't have is three weekends to work out which of four thousand tools fixes it, whether it talks to the system you already pay for, and — the part that stops most practices cold — whether it's even allowed near patient information.

So it stays on the list. And every week it stays there has a number attached.

That's the job. Not to make you an AI person. To hand you three decisions already made, by someone who understands both sides of the problem.

The prescription

What's actually in it

  • The three to seven bottlenecks costing you the most, quoted back in the words you used
  • A named tool for each, with its real monthly price on its real plan tier
  • Honest setup time — if it needs a developer, we say so and it doesn't make the list
  • For anything touching PHI: whether the vendor signs a BAA, which plan tier that takes, and what should never be entered into it
  • A four-day start plan, ten minutes a day, in order
  • The arithmetic: hours returned, at your hourly value, minus what the tools cost

Where a vendor's position on a BAA can't be verified, the report says "needs verification before use with patient data" rather than guessing. That line is the difference between advice you can act on and advice that creates a problem.

Who we are

We read the charts after it goes wrong. This is the other end of that job.

Frigeri & Co. is a small operations practice founded by Quinn Frigeri, RN — critical care, and medical-legal record review. The second one is why this exists.

Reviewing records for attorneys means reading a practice's documentation after something has already gone wrong. And the pattern is almost never one person having a bad day. It's a process nobody had time to fix: a follow-up with no owner, a form that lived in two places, a handoff that depended on somebody remembering. By the time it reaches a file, it's expensive in ways that have nothing to do with software.

So we read your week the way we'd read a record — for where it breaks, not for what to buy. That work also means years of handling protected health information under rules with teeth, which is why every recommendation here answers the data question instead of waving at it. Most people selling AI into practices have never heard of a BAA. That's how a clinic ends up with a compliance problem instead of an extra five hours.

We also build the systems we recommend, which is the difference between advice and a brochure. If a tool goes in your practice, we've set it up before — and if it can't do what the vendor's website claims, we'd rather find that out on our time than on yours.

Questions

The ones we get asked

Can we even use these tools with patient information?

Some, on specific plan tiers, with a signed BAA. Others never. That distinction is in the report for every recommendation, because it's the question that stops most practices from adopting anything — and the one most consultants answer wrong.

We're not lawyers and this isn't legal advice. We tell you what the vendor commits to in writing, and what we couldn't verify.

Do I have to learn any of this?

No. That's the point of a prescription — the decisions are already made. If you'd rather not do the setup either, we do that part.

We barely use technology. Is it too early for us?

It's the opposite. Most of what we recommend isn't AI at all — it's ordinary software you didn't know existed. The measurement is hours, not technology.

Is this going to replace our front desk?

No, and practices that try it usually make a mess. This is about the admin drag on the people you already have. Nobody's job gets smaller; their week does.

We tried something like this and it didn't work.

Usually a tool got dropped onto a broken process. That's why the first session is spent on how you actually work before anything is recommended, and why anything needing a six-week rollout doesn't make the report.

How does the guarantee work?

If the report can't identify at least five hours a week, you pay nothing and you keep the report. We'd rather refund than pad a number — the arithmetic is only worth anything if you can trust it.

How long does the whole thing take on our end?

Ninety minutes of your time, total: fifteen on the first call, forty-five on the audit, thirty on the walkthrough. Nothing to prepare, no documents to gather — we ask about the week you already have.

Are you reselling software?

No. No affiliate deals, no commissions, no vendor relationships. You buy directly at public prices. It's the only way a recommendation means anything.

Do you work outside Miami?

Yes — everything runs by phone and video, and most of our work is remote. Practices anywhere in the US.
