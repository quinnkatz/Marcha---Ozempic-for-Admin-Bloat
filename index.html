You have a business full of little leaks.

01

The lead waits

The front desk is with someone. The inquiry sits. By the time anyone is free, they’ve booked somewhere else.

02

Economics

The expensive part usually isn’t the software.

It’s what the current system keeps costing you.

  • A missed inquiry that would have become a $600 customer is not five minutes of phone time.
  • An empty $300 appointment is expired capacity. You cannot sell that hour again.
  • An owner who is the backup system is not a plan that scales.

Marcha measures the economic problem before recommending the technical solution.

    Do we need to know AI already?

    No. The first questions are about the week: what waits, what expires, what lands on the owner. A tool comes up only if it has a credible case.

    Will you build custom software?

    No. The assessment doesn’t ship an app. If something is worth building or configuring later, that work is separately scoped, and only after the economics say so.

    What does the assessment cost?

    A flat $1,999. Implementation is separately scoped. It is not included in the fee. If a change needs new software, that cost is written down before you decide. If the economics don’t support it, we say leave it alone.

    Is implementation included?

    No. Implementation is separately scoped. The $1,999 visit is the diagnosis and the roadmap: what to fix, in what order, what success looks like, and what not to spend on. Help putting it in place is a different conversation, after you have that picture.

    Will this replace my front desk?

    No. The point is to stop handing the team work a system could hold, and to leave the human work human. Your people stay the face of the business.

    Which software do you work with?

    Whatever runs the booking today: Zenoti, Boulevard, Mindbody, GlossGenius, Weave, NexHealth, and similar. We read that setup before we suggest anything new. We don’t start by making you switch.

    Who shows up on the calls?

    You work directly with Quinn, a registered nurse. If you later want help setting something up, and the economics support it, the team can join for that part.

    Can these tools touch client or health information?

    For medspas, dental, and outpatient practices, any tool that touches patient information needs a signed BAA on the right plan. After-hours note scribes always keep a clinician’s sign-off. We never recommend putting client health details into ChatGPT or similar. For most studios, the tools never touch health info at all. The report marks which is which and says “needs verification” instead of guessing. We’re not lawyers, this isn’t legal advice, and nothing here diagnoses a patient.

    Is my business too small for this?

    Often the smaller teams feel it most, because the person answering the phone is the same person doing the service. The work fits owner-led teams averaging 2–20 employees. If the fit call says the fee doesn’t match the problem, we’ll tell you.
