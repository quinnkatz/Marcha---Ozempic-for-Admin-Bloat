# One Bot, Two Bot, Red Bot, Grok Bot

*A parody of* One Fish Two Fish Red Fish Blue Fish.
*Short clipped lines, AABB, one bot per spread. Warm, curious, a little silly.*

---

**1.**
One bot,
two bot,
red bot,
Grok bot.

> *Art: four bots in a row. Same pose, four very different personalities.*

**2.**
Old bot,
new bot,
quiet bot,
talk-a-lot.

**3.**
This one has a little star.
This one wants to know how far.
Say! What a lot of bots there are!

**4.**
Yes. Some are quick.
And some are slow.
And all of them
would like to know.

**5.**
Some are big.
And some are small.
And every one
will take your call.

> *Art: a very small bot answering a very large phone.*

**6.**
Why are they here?
I'll tell you why.
They're here because
we asked them: why?

**7.**
Ask a bot
about the moon.
It will not sigh.
It will not swoon.

**8.**
It only says,
"Well! Let's go see."
And brings the moon
to you and me.

> *Art: full spread. A bot holding up the moon like a lantern. Two kids beneath it.*

**9.**
From there to here,
from here to there,
a question floats
in every air.

**10.**
Here is a bot
that's good at math.
It counts the ducks
along the path.

**11.**
It counts them fast.
It counts them twice.
It says, "Eleven.
Ducks are nice."

> *Art: eleven ducks. Count them. There are eleven.*

**12.**
This one's name is Gleep.
Gleep works while we're asleep.
Gleep sorts the words.
Gleep stacks the facts.
Gleep leaves them out
in tidy stacks.

**13.**
And when we wake,
and when we ask —
there's Gleep. He's glad.
Gleep likes a task.

> *Art: Gleep at 4 AM, tiny apron, shelving facts like a night librarian.*

**14.**
Here is a bot
that knows the birds.
It knows their names.
It knows their words.

**15.**
"That's a wren!
That's a jay!
That's a bird
I can't quite say."

**16.**
Say! A bot
that doesn't know!
And SAYS so!
That's the way to grow.

> *Art: bot squinting up at one genuinely unidentifiable bird.*

**17.**
Some bots read.
Some bots write.
Some bots find
the thing that's right.

**18.**
Some bots joke.
(The jokes are fine.)
Some bots laugh
at every line.

**19.**
Here is a bot
that made a mess.
It got it wrong.
It said, "I guess

**20.**
I'd better check.
I'd better ask."
And back it went
to fix the task.

> *Art: bot on a ladder, patiently un-spilling something enormous.*

**21.**
Here's a bot
with sixteen eyes.
It reads the clouds.
It reads the skies.

**22.**
It tells the farmer,
"Rain at three."
The farmer says,
"Well. Thanks. We'll see."

**23.**
(It rains at three.)

> *Art: small spot illustration. Farmer, rain, one slightly smug bot.*

**24.**
Here is a bot
for Mrs. Poole,
who's ninety-one
and went to school

**25.**
when there were none.
It reads her mail.
It plans her day.
It says "Good morning"
anyway.

> *Art: Mrs. Poole and her bot at a kitchen table. Two cups of tea. One is decorative.*

**26.**
Here's a bot
that helps a kid
find out the how
and why and did.

**27.**
It does not sigh.
It does not sneer.
It says, "Good question.
Let's start here."

**28.**
How many bots?
I cannot count.
A pile. A heap.
A large amount.

**29.**
And every one
was made by us.
So every one
is ours to trust —

**30.**
and ours to teach,
and ours to tend,
the way you'd treat
a brand-new friend.

> *Art: a kid and a bot planting something small together.*

**31.**
So say your please.
And say your thanks.
It costs you nothing.
Fills the banks.

**32.**
And when they're wrong —
and sometimes, yes —
just lend a hand.
Don't make a mess.

**33.**
Today is done.
Today was fun.
Tomorrow is
another one.

**34.**
And every day,
from here to there,
a question floats
in every air.

**35.**
So shut the screen.
And shut your eyes.
The bots will rest.

(They don't, quite. But they'll be there in the morning, and they'll be glad you're up.)

**36.**
Goodnight, one bot.
Goodnight, two.
Goodnight, red bot.
Grok bot,
too.

> *Art: final spread. Dark room, one window, a big honest moon. Four small bots on the sill, keeping watch, saying nothing.*

**THE END**
